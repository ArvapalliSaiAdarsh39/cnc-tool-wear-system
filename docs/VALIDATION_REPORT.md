## V1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CNC Sentinel is a **well-engineered, internally rigorous, physics + gradient-boosted-ML hybrid** whose *output contract* is strongly aligned with the reference literature but whose *input contract* is not: it predicts the **correct wear metric (flank wear VB, mm)**, terminates at the **exactly correct ISO 3685 limit (0.30 mm)** validated in [P3] §6.2, simulates the **canonical three-stage wear curve** whose break-in ceiling (0.045 mm) lands within 10 % of [P2]'s validated Initial-stage boundary (0.05 mm), and covers **every workpiece material family** [P3] §4.2 enumerates (Inconel 718, Ti-6Al-4V, AISI 1045/C45, 42CrMo4, AISI 316, aluminium 6061/7075, cast iron FG 15/CGI 450). Its measured point-estimate error (**MAE 0.0132 mm, RMSE 0.0177 mm**) is *numerically better* than [P2]'s best A-LWDRL result (0.023/0.031 mm) and its wear-band inference latency (**6.25 ms**) beats [P2]'s 12.8 ms edge target — but **both comparisons are self-referential and must not be presented as parity with [P2]**, because the ground truth is the system's own synthetic generator, not a measured tool.

The single most important finding is that **CNC Sentinel consumes no sensor signals whatsoever.** All three papers are, at their core, about *sensing*: [P1] is a direct machine-vision measurement chain (CMOS → k-means → grayscale → Robert's edge → Hough), [P2] fuses a PCB 356A16 triaxial accelerometer and a Kistler 9257B dynamometer at 1–5 kHz, and [P3] ranks dynamometers, accelerometers and AE sensors across 77 papers. CNC Sentinel's 23 features are **operator-entered setpoints plus deterministically derived physics quantities**, sampled at **0.0083 Hz (one sample per 2 minutes)** — roughly five orders of magnitude slower than [P2]'s acquisition. Its two quasi-signal features are the nearest thing to a sensor channel and both are compromised: `spindle_load_pct` is *computed* from specific cutting energy × MRR (a [P3] §4.4.5 power-proxy in spirit, never measured) and is **degenerate** — mean 0.43 %, below 1 % for 84.5 % of the corpus, and 0.013 % for Inconel 718; the three `rolling_*_std` "instability" features are, in the live app, produced by **a UI slider the operator drags** (`app.py:649-651`), not by measurement.

A second substantive defect is **train/serve skew**: at the shipped default *Process Steadiness = 100 %*, all three `rolling_*_std` features are served as exactly **0.0**, a value only **1.4 %** of training rows occupy. The live dashboard therefore queries the model in a near-empty region of feature space, and the split-conformal **marginal coverage guarantee does not transfer** to that operating point (conformal prediction requires exchangeability). Relatedly, the **fast-boot tier the live app actually ships has a conformal correction of exactly 0.0000 mm** and a raw band width of 0.125 mm — so on first load the displayed Q10–Q90 interval (measured: **0.068–0.206 mm** on an Inconel 718 scenario) carries **no calibration at all**, while the UI presents it as "Conformalized Uncertainty Bounds."

**Overall verdict: ⚠️ PARTIALLY VALIDATED.** Nothing in the system *contradicts* the reference papers; the engineering quality (run-level splitting, conformal calibration, isotonic probability calibration, SHAP grounded in a strict LLM payload contract, explicit decision-support-only guardrails) is above the median of the 77 papers [P3] reviews. But the system is a **physics-grounded what-if decision console**, not the **sensor-driven online TCM system** all three papers validate, and its headline accuracy claims are not externally comparable until it is evaluated on a real or public benchmark corpus (PHM 2010, which [P2] uses, is the obvious target). Economic justification — the easiest available validation win, since [P1] and [P3] both hand you citable figures — is **entirely absent** from the codebase.

| **What IS actually used** | 23 features | — | — | 3 categorical codes (material, coolant, coating) · 8 operator setpoints (RPM, feed mm/rev, ap, ae, workpiece Ø, flutes Z, spindles, nose radius) · 1 clock (elapsed runtime) · **8 deterministically derived physics quantities** (Vc, Taylor life, Taylor ratio, MRR, cumulative energy, fz, Ra, spindle load) · 3 slider-driven rolling stds. **All are inputs, none are process responses.** |

**Fundamental signal-direction finding.** [P3] §4.3 defines online measurement as *indirect*: sensors capture the **process output response** (force, vibration, AE, current), features are extracted, and AI infers wear. [P3] §4.5.3: "The sensors used to acquire the data provide the state of wear of the tool instant by instant." Every one of CNC Sentinel's 23 features is a **process input or a deterministic function of process inputs**. The system therefore does not perform indirect sensing at all — it performs **forward physics simulation with an ML correction layer**. That is a legitimate and useful artefact (it is what [P2] §I calls a "physical model"), but it is a **different epistemic category** from all three papers, and it cannot detect the phenomena sensors exist to detect: chatter, built-up edge, chipping, material hard-spot variation, or insert-to-insert lot variation. `data_generator.py` *simulates* per-tool variation (`TOOL_INSTANCE_WEAR_FACTOR_STD = 0.09`), but the live app has no channel through which real variation could enter.

**Sampling rate.** Measured: `SAMPLE_INTERVAL_MIN = 2.0` → **0.0083 Hz**. [P2] PHM 2010: **5 kHz**; [P2] real 45# steel: **1 kHz**; [P1]: image every **5 min**. The system sits at [P1]'s cadence but with none of [P1]'s measurement — and 5 orders of magnitude below [P2]. No frequency-domain analysis is possible at 0.0083 Hz, which fully explains (though does not excuse) the absence of FFT/wavelet/EMD/STFT features that [P3] §4.5.2 documents in 26 articles.

## V7. VALIDATED POINTS — WHAT IS CORRECTLY ALIGNED

All three papers converge on this metric; the system chose it. **(V2 #1, ✅)**

2. **The ISO 3685 replacement threshold is an exact numeric match, and correctly attributed.** `config.py:257` — `SCRAP_VB_THRESHOLD_MM = 0.30  # ISO 3685 average flank-wear tool-life criterion`. [P3] §6.2, p.40 verbatim: *"ISO 3685 indicates that the VB value is 0.3 mm."* The app renders it as a dashed red "Scrap threshold" line on the Tab-1 band chart and states the standard by name in Tab 3. This is the single cleanest literature-to-code trace in the repository. **(V2 #2, ✅)**

3. **The pre-threshold visual-inspection alert responds directly to a gap [P3] names.** `VISUAL_INSPECTION_VB_ALERT_MM = 0.25` fires *before* the hard ISO limit. [P3] §6.2 argues precisely for this: *"In industrial environments, every process is different… Some tools have a maximum wear of 0.1 mm, while others have a maximum wear of 0.4 mm. **ISO 3685 should be restated or adapted.**"* The system adapts it rather than treating 0.30 mm as absolute — an auditable, literature-motivated design decision. **(V2 #3, ✅)**

4. **The three-stage wear-progression structure and its break-in amplitude are validated.** `_wear_model` composes break-in (saturating exponential) + steady-state (linear in life fraction) + catastrophic (cubic past onset). [P2] §III.C validates a three-category scheme — Initial / Intermediate / Final — and [P2] Fig. 2 shows the same sigmoidal-with-accelerating-tail shape. The break-in asymptote **0.045 mm** sits within 10 % of [P2]'s Initial-stage ceiling **0.05 mm**. **(V2 #11, V5, ✅ structure)**

5. **Material coverage is a superset of every material [P3] enumerates.** [P3] §4.2 lists: cast irons, steel alloys (C45, stainless AISI 316, 42CrMo4, S235JR, 1018, 1040, mild, tempered, AISI M3:2), superalloys (titanium alloys, Inconel 718, Inconel 625), aluminium alloys (6061, 5053, 6082, 2024, 7022, 7075). `config.MATERIALS` covers **Al 6061-T6, Al 7075-T6, AISI 1045 (= C45/S45C), AISI 4140 (= 42CrMo4), AISI D2, AISI 304, AISI 316L, 17-4 PH, Ti-6Al-4V, Inconel 718, Gray Cast Iron FG 15 / CGI 450** — 11 profiles. **Every [P3] family is represented, including the two exact designations [P3] Table 2 singles out for cast iron (FG 15 in ref [70], CGI 450 in ref [75]) and the 42CrMo4/AISI 316 pairing.** [P2]'s two validated materials — **Inconel 718 and 45# steel** — are both present (45# ≈ AISI 1045).
