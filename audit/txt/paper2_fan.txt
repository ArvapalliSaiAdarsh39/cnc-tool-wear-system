A CNC Machine Tool Wear Prediction Model 
Integrating Deep Reinforcement Learning and Edge 
Data Processing 
Zhenduo Fan 
Heilongjiang Polytechnic, Harbin, Heilongjiang, 150080, China 
fan1988_05@163.com 
 
 
Abstract—This paper proposes a CNC machine tool wear 
prediction model integrating deep reinforcement learning and 
edge data processing, with attention-enhanced lightweight deep 
reinforcement learning (A-LWDRL) algorithm at its core. The 
model adopts a four-layer architecture: edge data preprocessing, 
attention feature extraction, lightweight DRL prediction, and 
edge-cloud collaborative optimization, with over 90% of the 
computational tasks completed at the edge. Adaptive noise 
reduction of industrial signals is achieved through a sliding 
window-based dynamic noise suppression algorithm, key 
features are selected using a time -series-channel dual -
dimensional attention mechanism, a lightweight network is 
constructed using depthwise separable convolution and inverted 
residual structures, and a dynamic hierarchical reward 
mechanism optimizes the model's convergence performance. 
Experimental verification shows that the model achieves mean 
absolute errors as low as 0.023mm and 0.028mm on the PHM 
2010 public dataset and the actual cutting dataset of 45# steel, 
respectively, with a wear level accuracy of 98.7%. The model has 
a storage capacity of only 2.3MB, a prediction latency of 12.8ms, 
and CPU and GPU utilization rates of 28% and 42%, 
respectively. Compared with traditional benchmark models 
such as DQN and LSTM, the prediction accuracy is improved 
by 23.4% -32.4%, and the parameter size is reduced by more 
than 89.7%. It effectively balances prediction accuracy, real -
time performance, and edge resource adaptability, providing an 
efficient technical solution for online monitoring of CNC 
machine tool wear. 
Keywords—CNC machine tool , tool wear prediction , deep 
reinforcement learning , edge computing, attention mechanism, 
lightweight network, edge-cloud collaboration 
I. INTRODUCTION 
As the core equipment of intelligent manufacturing, the 
tool wear state of CNC machine tools directly determines 
machining accuracy, production efficiency, and operational 
safety. Progressive wear of cutting  tools during the cutting 
process leads to workpiece dimensional deviations and 
surface quality degradation, and in severe cases, tool 
breakage and equipment downtime. Statistics show that tool 
failure accounts for over 35% of production line malfunctions, 
causing significant economic losses. With the deepening 
advancement of Industry 4.0 and intelligent manufacturing, 
the requirements for real -time and accurate tool condition 
monitoring in production lines have increased dramatically 
[1]. Traditional offline monitoring and periodic replacement 
methods can no longer meet the needs of flexible production, 
necessitating the construction of an efficient online wear 
prediction system. 
The widespread adoption of edge computing technology 
provides a new technological path for real-time monitoring of 
industrial equipment [2]. By processing data locally at the 
data generation point, it effectively reduces cloud 
transmission latency and bandwidth consumption, adapting 
to the real-time decision-making needs of CNC machine tools. 
Current tool wear prediction methods mainly fall into three 
categories: physical models, which construct wear evolution 
equations based on cutting mechanics principles, suffer from 
poor adaptability to complex working conditions and limited 
generalization ability; machine learning models (such as 
SVM and random forest) rely on manual feature engineering, 
resulting in insufficient fitting accuracy for nonlinear wear 
processes; and deep learning models (such as LSTM and 
CNN), while capable of autom atically extracting features, 
have large parameter scales, high computational 
requirements, and are difficult to deploy on resource-
constrained edge nodes. 
In recent years, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has 
gradually been applied to industrial prediction due to its 
advantages in temporal decision-making tasks [3]. However, 
existing fusion schemes still face significant bottlenecks: first, 
the complex DRL network structure and parameter 
redundancy make it difficult for edge nodes to support 
computational power, leading to prediction delays exceeding 
the threshold of industrial scenarios; second, vibration and 
cutting force signals in industrial environments contain a 
large amount of non- stationary noise, and existing 
preprocessing methods cannot a daptively suppress 
interference, affecting feature extraction accuracy; and third, 
traditional DRL reward mechanisms are simplistic and 
difficult to adapt to the multi -stage evolution characteristics 
of tool wear, resulting in slow model convergence and la rge 
prediction errors  [4]. These problems severely restrict the 
practical application of DRL combined with edge data 
processing in tool wear prediction. To address the 
aforementioned challenges, this paper proposes a CNC 
machine tool wear prediction model that integrates deep 
reinforcement learning and edge data processing. The core 
innovation is the Attention -Enhanced Lightweight Deep 
Reinforcement Learning (A -LWDRL) algorithm  [5]. This 
model suppresses noise interference through an adaptive edge 
data preprocessing module, selects key features based on an 
attention mechanism, adopts a lightweight network structure 
to adapt to edge resource constraints, and designs a dynamic 
hierarchical reward mechanism to optimize model 
convergence performance. Simultaneously, an edge -cloud 
collaborative architecture is constructed to achieve efficient 
iteration of model parameters and real -time inference  [6]. 
Experimental results show that this model significantly

II. OVERALL PREDICTION MODEL ARCHITECTURE AND 
ALGORITHM DESIGN 
A. Overall Model Framework 
The model employs a four -layer collaborative 
architecture to achieve a deep fusion of efficient data 
processing and accurate prediction at the edge. The edge data 
preprocessing layer is deployed on the local edge node of the 
CNC machine tool, performing real-time noise reduction and 
standardization on the raw vibration and cutting force signals; 
the attention feature extraction layer focuses on wear-related 
features, filtering redundant information to reduce 
computational overhead; the lightweight DRL prediction 
layer completes time-series prediction of wear amount based 
on optimized features; the edge -cloud collaborative 
optimization layer balances real- time performance and 
prediction accuracy through a closed -loop mechanism of 
cloud-initialized model parameters, online iterative training 
at the edge, and periodic parameter feedback from the cloud 
[7]. Data flow follows the logic of "acquisition - 
preprocessing - feature selection - prediction - optimization," 
with over 90% of computational tasks completed at the edge, 
and only key parameter updates relying on cloud interaction.

C. Model Training and Inference Process 
During the training phase, the A -LWDRL algorithm 
parameters are first initialized via a cloud server, including 
convolutional kernel weights, attention mechanism 
parameters, and initial values for the  DRL network. Edge 
nodes collect sensor signals in real time, and after 
preprocessing and feature selection, online training is 
performed based on a dynamic hierarchical reward 
mechanism, updating the network parameters every 100 time 
steps. The cloud receives training logs and parameter 
gradients uploaded by the edge nodes hourly, optimizes and 
adjusts global parameters through batch data, and feeds this 
optimization back to the edge nodes, achieving iterative 
parameter optimization  [11]. During the infere nce phase, 
edge nodes directly load the optimized model, perform real -
time prediction of the preprocessed features, and output tool 
wear amount and wear level. The inference latency is 
controlled within 13 ms, meeting the real -time monitoring 
requirements of industry. 
III. EXPERIMENTAL SIMULATION AND RESULT ANALYSIS 
A. Experimental Environment Setup 
The core of the hardware platform is an NVIDIA Jetson 
Xavier NX edge node, configured with an 8- core ARM 
Cortex-A78 CPU, a 384 -core CUDA GPU, 8GB LPDDR5 
memory, and 16GB eMMC storage to meet the lightweight 
computing requirements of the edge. The CNC machine tool 
used is a FANUC 0i-MF vertical machining center, equipped 
with a PCB 356A16 three-axis vibration sensor and a Kistler 
9257B piezoelectric cutting force sensor [12]. The former has 
a measurement range of ±50g and a sensitivity of 100mV/g, 
while the latter has a range of 0 -50kN and a nonlinear error 
of less than 0.5%, ensuring signal acquisition accuracy. The 
software environment is built on Python 3.9, using PyTor ch 
1.18 as the deep learning framework. TensorRT 8.4 is used 
for model quantization acceleration. Data processing relies 
on NumPy 1.24 and Pandas 2.0. Simulation visualization uses 
Matplotlib 3.7. All experiments were performed in a unified 
environment to ensure the comparability of results. 
B. Dataset Design and Preprocessing 
 The experiment employed dual validation using both 
publicly available and real -world datasets. The publicly 
available dataset was the PHM 2010 Tool Wear Challenge 
dataset, containing 5 different combinations of cutting 
parameters and full lifecycle data of 3 WC -Co tools cutting 
Inconel 718 alloy. The wear range was 0- 0.21 mm, with a 
sampling frequency of 5 kHz. After screening, the effective 
sample size was 8600 sets  [13]. The real -world dataset 
focused on the cutting scenario of 45# steel, using 3 identical 
WC-Co carbide tools at a fixed cutting speed of 120 m/min, 
feed rate of 0.15 mm/r, and depth of cut of 3 mm. Cutting 
continued until the wear reached 0.2 mm, with a sampling 
frequency of 1 kHz. A total of 12000 data sets were collected, 
covering vibration acceleration, triaxial cutting force signals, 
and corresponding tool wear (measured using an optical 
microscope with an accuracy of 0.001 mm). The data 
preprocessing workflow includes : removing outliers using 
the 3σ criterion; mapping the data to the [0,1] interval using 
Min-Max normalization; generating time -series samples 
using sliding window segmentation (window size 1024, step 
size 256); dividing the public dataset into training and test 
sets in a 7:3 ratio; and dividing the actual dataset in an 8:2 
ratio to ensure training effectiveness and validation reliability. 
C. Evaluation Indicators Selection 
The experiment selected five core evaluation indicators to 
comprehensively measure mode l performance: Mean 
Absolute Error (MAE) quantifies the average deviation 
between predicted and true values, reflecting overall 
prediction accuracy; Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) 
amplifies the impact of deviation through squared variance, 
highlighting the interference of large errors on model 
performance; Prediction Latency refers to the total time taken 
for a single sample from input to output, directly related to 
the feasibility of real- time applications at the edge; Model 
Storage Capacity is the size of the model file after TensorRT 
quantization, reflecting the storage adaptability of edge nodes; 
Wear Level Accuracy divides wear into three categories: 
initial (0 -0.05mm), intermediate (0.05 -0.15mm), and final 
(0.15-0.2mm), evaluating the model's classification and 
decision-making capabilities in actual production scenarios. 
D. Comparative and Ablation Experiment Design 
The comparative experiments selected five representative 
benchmark models: the traditional machine learning model 
SVM (using radial basis funct ion kernel), the classic deep 
learning temporal model LSTM (3 hidden layers, 128 
neurons), the hybrid deep learning model CNN-LSTM (CNN 
extracts spatial features combined with LSTM to capture 
temporal dependencies), the traditional deep reinforcement 
learning model DQN (original network structure), and the 
lightweight benchmark model CNN+MLP (depthseparable 
convolutions combined with multilayer perceptrons). All 
models were trained and optimized using the same 
hyperparameter range to ensure fairness in the comparison  
[14]. The ablation experiment design included three model 
variants: A-LWDRL with the attention module removed, A-
LWDRL with the dynamic reward mechanism removed, and 
A-LWDRL without lightweighting. By comparing with the 
complete model, the independent contributions of the 
attention-guided feature selection mechanism, the dynamic 
hierarchical reward mechanism, and the lightweight network 
structure were verified.

absolute error is 0.023 mm and the root mean square error is

traditional DQN, resp ectively; on the real dataset, the mean

devices [15]. The wear level accuracy reaches 98.7%, 
significantly better than all benchmark models, verifying the 
algorithm's ability to balance accuracy and lightweight design.

IV. CONCLUSION 
 The A-LWDRL tool wear prediction model proposed in 
this paper achieves an effective balance in prediction 
accuracy, real -time performance, and edge resource 
adaptability through the synergistic innovation of adaptive 
edge data preprocessing, attention feature selection, 
lightweight network design, and dynamic hierarchical reward 
mechanism. Experimental results show that the model 
exhibits optimal comprehensive performance on both public 
and real -world datasets, significantly improving prediction 
accuracy and reducing resource consumption and inference 
latency compared to traditional models. This fully verifies the 
feasibility and superiority of the fusion application of deep 
reinforcement learning and edge data processing. The 
limitations of this research lie in the fact that the experimental 
dataset mainly focuses on scenarios where WC -Co tools cut 
Inconel 718 alloy and 45 steel. Its adaptability to complex and 
extreme working conditions such as high -speed cutting and 
dry cutting has not been fully verifi ed. Furthermore, the 
adaptive adjustment coefficients and reward mechanism 
parameters in the model still rely on experience and data 
training optimization, lacking theoretical derivation support. 
Future research directions could extend to cross -condition 
prediction scenarios for multi -material cutting tools and 
multi-type workpieces, introducing transfer learning to 
improve model generalization capabilities; further optimizing 
attention mechanisms and lightweight network structures, 
and combining interpreta ble AI technology to enhance the 
transparency of model decisions; exploring the fusion path of 
digital twins and real- time monitoring data to construct an 
intelligent management and control system for the entire tool 
lifecycle, achieving the integration of  wear prediction and 
maintenance decisions.
